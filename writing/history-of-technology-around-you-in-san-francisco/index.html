History of the San Francisco Office

I turned the corner at the Exa office the other day and saw a monitor that had the word Stripe glowing. This used to be the Stripe office when it was just a few hundred people, before being used by OpenAI, SpaceX and eventually Exa. When we first moved in to the building, some leftover spaceX mattresses were still on the floor.

“History is literally present in all that we do.” James Baldwin once wrote and in San Francisco the history of technology feels very close to you.

You can't help but think: if that group of people was able to figure it out with the set of cards they were dealt, why can't we? Impossible outcomes doesn't seem unrealistic when you have examples all around you of people that have defied reality.

A few months after I had moved to San Francisco, I remember reading the Tinkering of Bob Noyce, about the founder of intel and the story of how the chip age turned Santa Clara into Silicon Valley. On a micro level, I felt how close the future he envisioned was in my immediate environment - the people behind superintelligence and global financial infrastructure spent all their time in the same rooms I do every day.

Even if none of us can recreate the journey they had, the idea that someone sitting in the same office walls cannot create meaningful things with outsized impact feels naive. Everytime I walk by the Stripe monitor in the office, I get a little jolt of how tangibly close that future feels to me in this city.
